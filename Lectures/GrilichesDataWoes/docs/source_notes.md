# Source Notes

## Griliches 1994

Primary paper:

Zvi Griliches, 1994, "Productivity, R&D, and the Data Constraint,"
American Economic Review 84(1): 1-23.

Local source:

- `originals/Griliches-ProductivityRDData-1994.pdf`

Public metadata:

- JSTOR stable page: https://www.jstor.org/stable/2117968
- NBER reprint page: https://www.nber.org/books-and-chapters/rd-and-productivity-econometric-evidence/productivity-rd-and-data-constraint

Digitized figure files from the older `Data/ZG` material are no longer part of
the active lecture bundle. The lecture now rebuilds the figures and tables from
public BEA, BEA-BLS, BEA NIPA archive, BEA Fixed Assets, and USPTO data.

## Current Data Sources

The active lecture bundle uses:

- BEA-BLS integrated industry-level production account workbooks for
  productivity, TFP, gross output, value added, labor hours, and R&D capital
  compensation.
- BEA-BLS historical KLEMS research tables for 1947-2016 productivity
  components.
- BEA NIPA archive Section 6 tables for national income by industry and persons
  engaged in production by industry.
- BEA Fixed Assets tables for R&D investment, IPP investment, and government
  fixed investment.
- USPTO patent activity and workload tables for patent application counts.

Older standalone BLS workbooks and derived BLS CSVs were removed from the
active path because the current Griliches lecture no longer uses them.

## Interpretation for the Monograph

The bundle is organized around a forward-in-time narrative:

1. Griliches' original evidence: the measurable economy shrinks, the residual
   remains large, R&D-productivity links are unstable, and patent productivity
   appears to decline.
2. Updated public-data evidence: more data are now available, but sectoral
   coverage, classification changes, quality adjustment, intangible capital,
   and service output remain central measurement problems.
3. Monograph pivot: better data help, but they do not eliminate latent objects.
   Theory is needed to discipline what cannot be directly observed.
